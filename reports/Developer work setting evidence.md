# Developer work setting evidence

Research date: 12 November 2026. Numbers below were checked against the original pages. Surveys are not averaged. A missing cut is written as "not found".

## A. Bottom line

The likely user is a person who already writes software or directs a coding agent, and who can talk because they are at home or in a room where speech does not bother other people. Developer surveys support a large remote minority and a large hybrid middle, while US government data support a real base of employed software developers and high telework in computer and mathematical occupations. A spoken desktop tool fits a home day. It is a weak fit on an office day, in a shared quiet room, in a meeting, or while commuting. The supported public claim is that remote and hybrid work are common among people who answer developer surveys, and that AI coding tools are already widely used. No survey found here says that developers want voice input, counts non-engineer "vibe coders," or measures a time cost for pressing Enter.

## C. Work setting

Developer surveys and worker surveys answer different questions.

Stack Overflow asks "Which best describes your current work situation?" In 2023, among 73,810 respondents, 41.41% said remote, 42.18% said hybrid, and 16.41% said in-person. In 2024 the same page says hybrid stayed at 42% and in-person rose to 20%, and it compares that with 16% in 2023 and 15% in 2022. The 2024 paragraph does not restate the remote share. In 2025 the answer list changed, and the text extract confirms the response count, 33,686, plus a US remote share of 45% and a German "your choice" share of 21%. It does not confirm a full global five-way table, so none is printed here. In 2026, under the five answers, 34.8% said remote and 17.6% said in-person. The two hybrid answers are 19.9% and 15.5%, and 12.2% said the schedule was their choice. The page's own analysis says US remote work is 48% and French remote work is 21%, with India's in-person share the highest of the countries it names, at 28%. These are self-selected global respondents, not a professional-only cut.

JetBrains, a vendor survey, asks where developers primarily work. In 2025 the published distribution is 22% only remotely, 22% mainly remotely, 17% equally, 21% mainly from the office, 17% only from the office, and 1% somewhere else. In 2026 the prose says 42% work remotely and 38% work mainly or entirely from the office. Those two figures are not a complete three-way split. GitHub Octoverse has no home-versus-office statistic.

No developer survey opened here says how many weekdays are at home. "Mainly remotely" and "leans heavy to flexibility" are not 1, 2, or 3 days.

For US workers in general, the October 2026 SWAA wave says about 27% of full paid days were worked from home. FRED prints 27.06, against 28.05 in October 2023. Over the year ending October 2026, 12% of full-time wage and salary employees were fully remote, 26% hybrid, and 62% on site. The authors' all-worker mean is 1.3 to 1.5 home days per week, and that mean includes people who are never home. About half of full-time employees with a four-year or graduate degree worked from home at least one day in the reference week. A distribution of hybrid workers across 1, 2, and 3 home days was not found.

Return-to-office rules have tightened on paper. The Q3 2025 Flex Index says average required office time for corporate employees rose from 2.57 to 2.87 days per week, and that 67% of structured hybrid firms require 3 office days. The same report says measured attendance rose 1% to 3% and that full home days in the SWAA stayed fairly stable. The FRED points agree that the US day-share barely moved. That is company policy for corporate employees, not observed attendance by software developers.

Developers are not a separate row in the CPS telework table. People at work in computer and mathematical occupations teleworked at 65.4% in the 2025 annual averages, including 36.1% who teleworked all hours. The broader professional group printed just above that row teleworked at 32.0%. Those are shares of people who teleworked at any time in a reference week. They are not comparable to the SWAA share of full days.

## D. Fit for saywhen

### Supported

- A public page can say that, in the 2026 Stack Overflow survey, 34.8% of people who described their current work situation said it was remote, and the survey's analysis puts the United States at 48%. Source row: Stack Overflow 2026 work setting.
- It can say JetBrains' 2025 chart shows 22% working only remotely and 22% mainly remotely, and that this is a JetBrains survey. Source row: JetBrains 2025.
- It can say the United States had 1,687,890 wage-and-salary software developers in May 2025, and a separate outlook base of 1,717,800 for that occupation in 2025. Source rows: BLS OEWS and the Occupational Outlook Handbook.
- It can say people in US computer and mathematical occupations were likely to have teleworked during the 2025 reference week: 65.4%. Source row: CPS table 60. The sentence has to keep the occupation name. It cannot say "software developers."
- It can say that, of the people who answered Stack Overflow's 2026 AI question, 65.9% use an AI coding assistant or coding agent. Source row: Stack Overflow 2026 AI. The base is 17,464 answers, not every developer.
- It can say, and should label as a vendor survey, that JetBrains found about 39% of professional developers using Claude Code at work in May to July 2026, 12% using Cursor, and 90% using a coding agent at least weekly. Source rows: JetBrains 2026 agent posts.
- It can say a home day is a common arrangement for US full-time employees with a degree: about half did at least one full home day in the SWAA year ending October 2026. Source row: SWAA. That row is not a developer row.

### Not supported

- A count of non-engineer "vibe coders." No survey uses that term as a population. The nearest Stack Overflow sentence says 72% of 2025 respondents are not vibe coding in their professional work, plus 5% who are emphatic about that.
- "Most developers want voice input," or any adoption rate for dictation while coding.
- "Most developers work from home." In the newest Stack Overflow table, remote is 34.8%. In-person is 17.6%. The rest are hybrid or flexible, which still includes office days.
- A developer-specific number of home days per week, including any claim that hybrid engineers are home two or three days.
- Treating GitHub's 225 million accounts, or India's 32.0 million GitHub accounts, as professional developers in those countries.
- Treating "more than 40% of pull requests" as "40% of developers use an agent."
- Treating DORA's 90% "AI at work" as coding-assistant adoption or as demand for speech.
- A measured time saving from removing a hotkey or from not pressing Enter.
- A share of developers who have a private room. Leesman's 58% and Gensler's 21% are other populations, and the Gensler figure is from 2020.

### Hybrid

On a home day, a hybrid developer is in the setting the product is built for: looking at a screen, able to talk, with a preview they can cancel. The surveys show that this kind of schedule exists. They do not show how many weekdays it covers. Stack Overflow's hybrid and "your choice" answers are large, and JetBrains' "equally" and "mainly" rows are large, but none of them is a day count. The Flex Index says the common written rule at structured hybrid US companies is 3 required office days. That is a rule for corporate employees, and the same report says attendance did not move as much as the rule.

An office day is a different product problem. The microphone and the webcam start listening because a face is at the screen and the lips are moving. In a quiet open office, that is awkward even when the words are only a command to a terminal. Gensler's summer 2020 survey of US office workers at larger employers found 69% in some form of open office and 21% in a mostly or totally private workplace. That is not a software-engineer sample, and it is six years old, so it cannot be quoted as the developer's office. It does show why an office day should not be described as if it were a home day. Meetings, a shared room, and the commute are also poor fits: the app is a desktop tool for the focused window, and no survey here measures developers talking to a computer in those settings. The honest hybrid story is a tool for the days they are home, left unused on the days they are sitting with other people.

## E. Honest pitch lines

In the 2026 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, 34.8% of the 24,602 people who described their current work situation said it was remote. [Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026]

That survey's analysis says remote work was 48% in the United States and 21% in France. [Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026]

JetBrains' 2025 chart of where developers primarily work shows 22% only remotely and 22% mainly remotely. [JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2025]

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 1,687,890 wage-and-salary software developers in May 2025. [US Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics]

Of the 17,464 people who answered Stack Overflow's 2026 question about AI at work, 65.9% said they use an AI coding assistant or coding agent. [Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026]

## F. Gaps

- United States, Europe, India, and Canada professional-developer headcounts from SlashData: not found. Evans 2025 country tables: survey exists but I could not open them.
- Whether the outlook's 1,717,800 includes the self-employed: not found on the page.
- Stack Overflow 2024 remote percentage in prose: not found. The page prints hybrid 42% and in-person 20%.
- Stack Overflow 2025 global five-way percentages in the text extract: not found. The page does print the US 45% remote line and the response count.
- Professional-developers-only work-location table: not found in Stack Overflow.
- Developer survey of 1, 2, and 3 home days: not found. Probably never measured in the surveys that were opened.
- Mean home days among hybrid workers only, in the newest SWAA: not found.
- Software developers inside CPS or SWAA: not found. Computer and mathematical occupations are the closest CPS row.
- A 2026 Flex Index report: not found.
- Non-engineer vibe coders: probably never measured. Stack Overflow asked whether vibe coding is part of professional work and published the "no" side.
- Voice input or dictation while coding, as a share of developers: not found. Probably never measured in Stack Overflow, JetBrains, or Octoverse. Small lab and interview studies exist.
- Developers who say they want voice input: not found.
- Developers in a private room or alone for part of the week: not found. Leesman and Gensler measure other workers.
- Cost of a hotkey, of pressing Enter, or of switching back to the keyboard for a short command while coding: probably never measured.
- Canada and Europe totals for professional developers: not found. GitHub's September 2026 country table does not include Canada or a Europe total.

## Strongest sources, and the number most likely to be misused

The three strongest sources are the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026 work-setting table, the May 2025 BLS employment count for software developers together with the 2025 CPS telework row for computer and mathematical occupations, and the November 2026 SWAA update with the FRED series of the share of full paid days worked from home.

The number most likely to be misused is JetBrains' "90% of professional developers were using AI coding agents at work at least weekly" in May to July 2026. It is a vendor survey, it is weekly use of a coding agent, and it says nothing about speech.
