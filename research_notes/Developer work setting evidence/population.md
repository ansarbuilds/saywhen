# Professional software developer population counts

Research date: 12 November 2026. Only figures found on pages opened for this note are included. Sources are not averaged. No market-size estimate is made.

## How many professional software developers do SlashData and Evans Data report?

### Takeaway

SlashData's own pages, opened here, state a worldwide total of 47.2 million developers at the beginning of 2025, of which 36.5 million are called professional, then a later total of 48.4 million updated Q3 2025 and 50 million active software developers as of Q1 2026. A verbatim glossary definition that separates professional from amateur or hobbyist was not on those pages. Evans Data's 2025 Worldwide Developer Population Report exists and says it estimates developers by country, but the population tables are marked redacted. The newest Evans headline that was actually readable is 27 million worldwide developers in 2024, and that press release does not define professional versus hobbyist.

### Cited Findings

#### SlashData total, beginning of 2025

- **Claim:** SlashData estimates 47.2 million developers worldwide at the beginning of 2025.
- **Number:** 47.2 million in the page title and section heading. The transcript body says "just over 47 million."
- **Year:** Beginning of 2025. Page dated 2 May 2025, updated 8 May 2025. Compared on the same page with "just over 31 million" in Q1 2022.
- **Population:** Global developer population. The page also splits this into a professional segment and an amateur segment (see the next statistic). It does not say the 47.2 million is professional-only.
- **Geography:** Worldwide.
- **Source name:** SlashData, transcript of "Global developer population trends 2025 - How many developers are there?"
- **URL:** https://www.slashdata.co/post/global-developer-population-trends-2025-how-many-developers-are-there
- **Sample size or method:** "independent bottom-up methodology" using two pillars: GitHub accounts, Stack Overflow accounts, and employment statistics from the USA and the European Union; plus the Global Developer Survey, "29 waves" with "more than 10,000 developers" in each wave. The page says employment statistics from the EU and USA are not extrapolated to other regions. Regional numbers use survey measurements of geographic distribution.
- **Caveat:** The same publisher later states 48.4 million (Q3 2025) and 50 million (Q1 2026). Those are later reference periods, not a correction printed on this page. A duplicate of this transcript is at https://research.slashdata.co/blog/global-developer-population-trends-2025-how-many-developers-are-there (dated 2 May 2025). Secondary blogs that repeat "47 million" were not used.
- **Definition quote:** "We calculate the number of active software developers globally using our own independent bottom-up methodology, firmly rooted in reliable measurement through our Global Developer Survey." Later on the same page, in a discussion of no-code tools: "In our survey, we use “developer” in a broad sense—anyone involved in software development projects in any capacity."

- **Number:** 36.5 million professional developers at early 2025. 21.8 million professional developers at early 2022. Amateur decline: "over 1 million" in the last year. An amateur headcount was not stated.
- **Year:** Early 2025 for 36.5 million. Early 2022 for 21.8 million. The page says the professional change was 70 percent.
- **Population:** "professional developers" versus "amateur developers" / "the amateur segment." The page calls amateurs "the traditional feeder population."
- **Geography:** Worldwide.
- **Source name:** SlashData, same 2 May 2025 transcript.
- **URL:** https://www.slashdata.co/post/global-developer-population-trends-2025-how-many-developers-are-there
- **Sample size or method:** Same bottom-up method as the 47.2 million total.
- **Caveat:** No sentence on this page defines professional as employed, paid, or excluding students and hobbyists. Do not treat 36.5 million as the Q3 2025 or Q1 2026 professional count. Those later pages do not repeat the professional split.
- **Definition quote:** "From early 2022 to early 2025, the number of professional developers grew significantly—by 70%—from 21.8 million to 36.5 million. By contrast, the population of amateur developers only grew moderately and actually declined by over 1 million in the last year."

### Inferences

- SlashData's 36.5 million professional figure and its 47.2 million, 48.4 million, and 50 million totals are different reference points. The later two totals are not labeled professional-only, so they should not be substituted for 36.5 million.
- Evans and SlashData are far apart on the totals that could be read (27 million in 2024 versus SlashData's 47.2 million at the start of 2025). The pages do not show that they count the same people. SlashData's readable definition is broad ("anyone involved in software development projects in any capacity"). Evans's 27 million page does not define the term.

### Gaps

- A verbatim SlashData definition of professional versus amateur or hobbyist was not found on the pages opened. The words are used, and one broad definition of "developer" is quoted above.
- No SlashData professional-only update after early 2025 was found. The Q3 2025 and Q1 2026 pages state totals only.
- Evans 2025 and 2026 worldwide totals: the 2025 report exists but I could not open it. A 2026 Evans population report was not found.
- An amateur headcount corresponding to the "over 1 million" decline was not stated.

Evans's readable 2024 release and redacted 2025 report page do not give those four cuts. GitHub later publishes United States and India counts of people on GitHub. Those are recorded in the Octoverse section and are not professional employment.

### Cited Findings

#### SlashData regions at the beginning of 2025

- **Claim:** Western Europe and North America each have about 9.5 million developers. South Asia has 7.5 million, up from 4 million in 2022, "largely driven by India's" tech workforce. Greater China has 5.8 million, up from 2.4 million since 2022. South America has 3.4 million, up from 1.7 million over three years.
- **Number:** Western Europe about 9.5 million. North America about 9.5 million. South Asia 7.5 million. Greater China 5.8 million. South America 3.4 million.
- **Year:** The geographic section sits in the beginning-of-2025 webinar. South Asia is "7.5 million today" against "4 million developers in 2022." The page does not say these regions are professional-only.
- **Population:** "developers" / "communities." Not labeled professional.
- **Geography:** Western Europe, North America, South Asia, Greater China, South America. India is mentioned as a driver of South Asia, not as its own number.
- **Source name:** SlashData, 2 May 2025 transcript.

## US Bureau of Labor Statistics employment

### Takeaway

For the occupation Software Developers, the May 2025 OEWS employer survey counts 1,687,890 wage-and-salary jobs. The Occupational Outlook Handbook, last modified 27 August 2026, lists a separate 2025 employment base of 1,717,800 for SOC 15-1252 and a projected 1,892,600 in 2035. The handbook's headline "1,905,400" jobs in 2025 is the combined profile of software developers plus software quality assurance analysts and testers, not software developers alone. Canada, Europe, and India are outside BLS national employment statistics.

### Cited Findings

#### OEWS employment, May 2025, Software Developers

- **Claim:** May 2025 national OEWS employment for Software developers is 1,687,890.
- **Number:** 1,687,890 employment. Same row: mean hourly wage $71.20, mean annual wage $148,100, median hourly wage $65.38.
- **Year:** May 2025. News release page last modified 15 May 2026. Table title: "Table 1. National employment and wage data from the Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics survey by occupation, May 2025."
- **Population:** Wage and salary employment in the occupation Software developers. This is employment, not a projection.
- **Geography:** United States.
- **Source name:** US Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics.
- **URL:** https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ocwage.htm
- **Sample size or method:** "employer survey measuring occupational employment and wage rates for wage and salary workers in nonfarm establishments in the United States." The opened page does not state the survey sample size.
- **Caveat:** Excludes the self-employed. This count is lower than the outlook handbook's 2025 employment base of 1,717,800. The pages opened do not explain that gap. Do not average the two figures. A fetch of https://www.bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oes151252.htm did not return an occupation profile. It returned the OEWS program home page, so that URL is not used as the source of 1,687,890.
- **Definition quote:** "The Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) survey is an employer survey measuring occupational employment and wage rates for wage and salary workers in nonfarm establishments in the United States." ## Stack Overflow, JetBrains, and GitHub Octoverse

### Takeaway

Stack Overflow 2025 and JetBrains Developer Ecosystem 2025, on the pages opened, publish survey respondent counts, not a population of developers. JetBrains describes a country model limited to professional developers but does not state a worldwide total on the methodology page opened. GitHub Octoverse does publish population-style counts of people on GitHub: more than 180 million in the 2025 report and more than 225 million in the 2026 report. GitHub defines a developer as anyone with a GitHub account.

### Cited Findings

#### Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025, respondents only

- **Claim:** The 2025 results use 49,009 responses from 177 countries. This is a sample, not a population estimate.
- **Number:** 49,009 responses. Not a population. About 15,000 additional responses "were not used."
- **Year:** Fielded 29 May 2025 to 23 June 2025.
- **Population:** Survey respondents who passed an age question, a consent question, and "Which of the following options best describes you today?"
- **Geography:** 177 countries. No population weights that produce a world or country headcount were stated on this page.
- **Source name:** Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025, Methodology.
- **URL:** https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/methodology
- **Sample size or method:** "Respondents were recruited primarily through channels owned by Stack Overflow." A Reddit ad campaign "accounted for < 2% of total responses." The page says highly engaged Stack Overflow users were more likely to see the prompts.
- **Caveat:** Do not treat 49,009 as the number of developers. A 2026 Stack Overflow methodology or press page was not opened in this pass, so no 2026 response count is recorded. No Stack Overflow page opened here estimates the world population of developers.
- **Definition quote:** "49,009 responses from 177 countries are used in these survey results."

#### JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2025, respondents only

- **Claim:** The 2025 report is based on 24,534 developers after data cleaning.
- **Number:** 24,534 people, also stated as "24,534 developers after data cleaning." Not a population.
- **Year:** Survey ran from April to June 2025.
- **Population:** Survey respondents.
- **Geography:** "across the globe." The opened landing page did not list country respondent counts in the text extracted.
- **Source name:** JetBrains, State of Developer Ecosystem Report 2025.
- **URL:** https://devecosystem-2025.jetbrains.com/
- **Sample size or method:** "We balanced our responses by geography, employment, programming languages, and JetBrains product use." "Some bias may remain, as JetBrains users could have been more likely to respond."
- **Caveat:** This is the cleaned sample. It is not an estimate of how many developers exist.
- **Definition quote:** "This report reveals trends and insights in the developer community and is based on the responses of 24,534 people across the globe."

#### JetBrains Data Playground model, no worldwide total on the page

- **Claim:** JetBrains describes a model that estimates developers by country and says the model counts only professional developers. No worldwide total is stated on the page opened.
- **Number:** not found for a worldwide or country headcount. The page's worked example is a formula for Python users in the USA in 2024, not a computed count.
- **Year:** The example uses "the USA in 2024" and "State of Developer Ecosystem 2024." The page says the survey reaches out to 30,000 developers worldwide each year. That 30,000 is the survey reach, not a population.
- **Population:** "professional developers." "We don’t include students or hobbyists."
- **Geography:** Country model. Canada is named only as an example of a national statistics source ("we use Statistics Canada for Canada"). No Canada count is given. The US input list is a set of occupations, not a headcount.
- **Source name:** JetBrains, Developer Ecosystem: Data Playground, Methodology.
- **URL:** https://lp.jetbrains.com/devecosystem-data-playground/methodology
- **Sample size or method:** Labor statistics (Eurostat and national statistics) plus the annual Developer Ecosystem survey. Density is developers divided by population, then applied to UN population figures. Missing countries use a 10 percent quantile of densities in the same region. "The numbers reflect only the most probable value (mean forecast) and shouldn’t be treated as an exact value." Retrospective mean absolute percentage error: "on average, the true values deviate from the forecasted ones by 14%."
- **Caveat:** The dashboard that would show the modelled counts was not opened. No JetBrains worldwide population number is recorded. The US occupation list on this page still includes older titles "15-1132 Software Developers, Applications" and "15-1133 Software Developers, Systems Software," plus web developers, database roles, testers, operations research analysts, statisticians, and data scientists. That is a wider set than BLS SOC 15-1252.
- **Definition quote:** "Our model currently considers only professional developers. We don’t include students or hobbyists as our data and methodology are not sufficient for counting these individuals."

#### GitHub Octoverse 2025, developers on GitHub

- **Claim:** More than 180 million developers work and build on GitHub. More than 36 million developers joined in the Octoverse year.
- **Number:** "180 million-plus" and "more than 180 million developers." Flow: "over 36 million" new developers in the past year, also "More than 36 million developers joined GitHub in a single year."
- **Year:** Page dated 28 October 2025, updated 28 February 2026. Glossary: "2025: Refers to September 1, 2024 through August 31, 2025."
- **Population:** Anyone with a GitHub account. This is a platform population, not professional software developers and not a survey sample.
- **Geography:** Worldwide. India added "more than 5.2 million developers in 2025." A regional table of net new developers, 2024 to 2025: APAC +13 million, LATAM +3.2 million, Europe +6.3 million (stand-out markets Germany, United Kingdom, France), Africa and the Middle East +3.4 million. ## The widely cited 47 million figure

### Takeaway

47.2 million, and the spoken "just over 47 million," are on SlashData's own 2 May 2025 page, with the method described above. The figure is not rejected. It is an early-2025 total of developers in SlashData's broad definition, and SlashData's own later pages state 48.4 million for Q3 2025 and 50 million for Q1 2026. Blogs that cite 47 million without that SlashData page were not used as sources.

### Cited Findings

- **Claim:** A rounded "47 million developers" figure is usable only when it is tied to SlashData's own transcript, which states 47.2 million at the beginning of 2025.
- **Number:** 47.2 million on the SlashData page. "47 million" with no source table: rejected if it appears only on a blog that does not link that page.
- **Year:** Beginning of 2025.
- **Population:** SlashData global developer population, not the professional-only 36.5 million, and not the later 48.4 million or 50 million totals.
- **Geography:** Worldwide.
- **Source name:** SlashData.
- **URL:** https://www.slashdata.co/post/global-developer-population-trends-2025-how-many-developers-are-there
- **Sample size or method:** See the first SlashData statistic. The original page was opened. It is not a blog that merely repeats an unlinked table.
- **Caveat:** Using a bare "47 million" as if it were the 2026 professional population would drop the year, the 47.2 precision, the professional split, and SlashData's later totals.
- **Definition quote:** Section heading: "There are 47.2 million developers in the world in 2025." Body: "at the beginning of this year, we estimate the global developer population at just over 47 million."

### Inferences

- None beyond the caveat above. The three SlashData vintages should stay side by side: 47.2 million at the start of 2025 (36.5 million professional), 48.4 million updated Q3 2025 (split not stated), and 50 million active software developers in Q1 2026 (split not stated).

### Gaps
