# Knowledge-worker and all-worker work from home

Research date: 12 November 2026. Scope is fully remote, hybrid, and office-only work for populations other than software developers, unless a source itself breaks developers out. Every labeled figure below is knowledge workers only when the source uses that term. None of the primary sources opened here use the label "knowledge workers." Closest published populations are all workers, wage and salary workers, college-educated workers, named occupation groups, and corporate employees at surveyed firms.

Statistics are not averaged across surveys. Policy counts are not treated as attendance. Geography is United States unless the row says otherwise.

## Newest SWAA wave: paid days at home, and fully remote, hybrid, and on-site shares

### Takeaway

The newest Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes (SWAA) wave included in the 5 November 2026 update is October 2026. About 27 percent of full paid days in the United States that month were worked from home. Over the twelve waves from November 2025 through October 2026, 12 percent of full-time wage and salary employees were fully remote, 26 percent were hybrid, and 62 percent were full-time on site. Those two results use different populations and denominators. A multi-country survey from the same research group, fielded November 2024 to February 2025, puts college-educated employees in English-speaking countries, including the United States, at about 1.5 to 1.9 full work-from-home days per week, against roughly one day per week in the global wording of the published paper. No newer global wave was found.

### Inferences

- The October 2026 day-share and the 12/26/62 person-split cannot be added or averaged. One is a share of full paid days in a broader worker sample. The other is a share of full-time wage and salary employees.
- The United States sits in the higher G-SWA group, but the opened text does not publish a US-only G-SWA days figure separate from Canada, the United Kingdom, and Ireland.

### Gaps

- A single US-only G-SWA days-per-week number, distinct from the English-speaking range, was not found in the text that could be read.
- The global "roughly 1 day" and "roughly 1.3 days" phrases were both printed by the authors. They were not reconciled.
- Exact bar values for the November 2025 to October 2026 chart of desired versus actual days per week were not recovered. The sample note for that chart was recovered: full-time wage and salary employees who worked 5 or more days in the reference week, passed attention checks, and had work-from-home experience during the pandemic. - Industry percentages on the November 2026 "finance, tech, and professional and business services" slide were not recovered as labeled numbers. See the FRED Information-sector series under the occupation section.
- Pre-COVID SWAA comparison level was described in the November 2026 notes as the 2019 ATUS share of diary days with at least 6 hours of paid work that also had at least 6 hours of paid work at home. The numeric pre-COVID share was not extracted.

## How the SWAA figures changed from 2023 to the newest wave

### Takeaway

Show both published levels. Do not convert them into a new percent change. October 2023 was about 28 percent of paid days at home (FRED 28.05). October 2026 was about 27 percent (FRED 27.06). The person split moved from 12.0 percent fully remote, 30.2 percent hybrid, and 57.8 percent fully on site in the August to November 2023 pool, to 12 percent, 26 percent, and 62 percent in the November 2025 to October 2026 pool. A second 2023 window, October to December 2023, is 12.4, 29.5, and 58.1. In the authors' annual education table, 2026 year-to-date day-shares are lower than 2023 for people with any college and unchanged for high school or less. The windows and populations differ, so this is not one trend line.

## BLS telework and work at home

### Takeaway

For 2025, an 11-month CPS annual average, 22.4 percent of people at work teleworked or worked at home for pay at any time in the reference week. Among them, 11.9 percent teleworked some but not all hours and 10.5 percent teleworked all hours. Wage and salary workers, excluding the incorporated self-employed, were at 20.9 percent. CPS measures hours, not days per week. The 2025 American Time Use Survey, released 25 June 2026, says 35 percent of employed people did some or all of their work at home on days they worked. That diary measure counts any home work, including partial days, and people can be in both the home and workplace columns.
